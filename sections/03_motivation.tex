\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motiv}

\subsection{PIM--Host Crossover}
\label{sec:bg:roofline}

% P:mot-roofline-setup
Throughput numbers in this section are modelled on the mobile platform and the edge server of
Table~\ref{tab:params}, which Section~\ref{sec:method} details. Fig.~\ref{fig:roofline} plots skinny-GEMM throughput
against $M$ in the roofline form~\cite{roofline} for the host NPU's roof and for simulated PIM GEMMs of stock AiM and
each platform's rival; each PIM curve is the geometric mean over the FC shapes of the platform's models.
Fig.~\ref{fig:roofline} and the later throughput figures plot $M = 2^k$. The mobile NPU is the Snapdragon 8 Gen
3's~\cite{sd8g3}, which reaches 56\,GB/s~\cite{powerinfer2} of its own 68\,GB/s pins~\cite{heterollm}; applied to the
51.2\,GB/s pins of x64 LPDDR5-6400, that fraction gives the NPU 42\,GB/s\ASSUMPTION. The edge-server NPU is
P3-LLM's~\cite{p3llm} (Table~\ref{tab:params}).

% P:mot-host-roof
\paragraph{Host roof in the skinny range}
The host stays memory-bound across the whole skinny range. The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3~\cite{sd8g3} NPU reaches its compute
roof only at $M \approx 143$, and P3-LLM's~\cite{p3llm} NPU reaches its roof at $M = 128$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:roofline}a,
b). Each NPU's ridge lies far above $M = 32$, so every skinny GEMM runs on the host's memory roof, which rises linearly
with $M$.

% P:mot-stock-crossover
\paragraph{Stock-PIM crossover}
Stock PIM beats the host only for the first few token rows. At $M = 1$ every PIM ceiling starts 9.75$\times$ above the
mobile NPU's roof and 4$\times$ above the edge-server NPU's roof. Stock AiM's ceiling is flat from $M = 1$, however,
because stock AiM repeats the GEMV once per token row: its MAC command count is $M$ times the number of weight columns,
and in our simulation its GEMM time grows in proportion to $M$. The rising host roof overtakes that ceiling, and stock
AiM wins a whole decode step only up to $M = 6$ on the mobile platform and $M = 2$--3 on the edge server, below most of
the published values of $M$ (Section~\ref{sec:bg:skinny}).

% P:mot-gemv-only
\paragraph{GEMV-only use of PIM}
Stock PIM is a GEMV engine, and published systems keep PIM for GEMVs and send the GEMMs to the host.
AttAcc~\cite{attacc} runs batched FC layers on the xPU and attention on PIM. NeuPIMs~\cite{neupims} maps the GEMMs to
the NPU because PIM ``lacks the computational power to handle GEMM effectively.'' IANUS~\cite{ianus} chooses NPU or PIM
per FC layer. P3-LLM~\cite{p3llm} offloads its linear layers to the NPU from batch 8, where its PIM becomes
compute-bound. Keeping PIM for GEMVs leaves the cause of stock PIM's flat GEMM ceiling unexamined, so
Section~\ref{sec:bg:cmd} splits a stock GEMM's time to locate that cause.

\subsection{Two Root Causes of Stock PIM Inefficiency}
\label{sec:bg:cmd}

% P:mot-time-breakdown
\paragraph{Useful-MAC share of stock GEMM time}
A stock GEMM's time divides into MAC slots and the non-MAC commands between them. Each all-bank MAC command occupies
one MAC slot, which holds useful and idle beats (Fig.~\ref{fig:dram_pim}b); the non-MAC commands are the row-switch
commands and the token load/drain commands of each row sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:dram_pim}c), and refresh.
Fig.~\ref{fig:breakdown} splits stock AiM's GEMM time at $M = 8$ on one channel of each platform for two kernels: one
tile, whose weights fill one row sweep in each bank and whose inputs fill one GB chunk ($K \times N$ of
$2048 \times 64$ on the mobile platform and $4096 \times 256$ on the edge server), and the $4096 \times 4096$ query
projection (q\_proj) of Llama-2-7B on the mobile platform and of Llama-3.1-8B on the edge server. The useful share of
the time is the useful-MAC utilization $U = W \tck / T_\mathrm{GEMM}$, where $W$ is the number of useful MAC beats per
PU and $T_\mathrm{GEMM}$ is the GEMM time; the reference PU of $U$ runs one useful beat per CK cycle, the $D$ beats per
column period of Section~\ref{sec:bg:pim}. Useful MAC work fills at most a fifth of the time: across the four kernels,
useful beats take 14.1--19.1\,\%, idle beats 42.2--57.4\,\% and the non-MAC commands 23.4--43.7\,\%.
$U$ is the product of a slot factor and a sweep factor, $U = \UMAC \times \fMAC$: the slot factor \UMAC is the useful
share of the MAC slots, and the sweep factor \fMAC is the share of the GEMM time spent in MAC slots. Stock AiM thus
loses useful MAC beats to two root causes: idle beats inside the MAC slots, which set the slot factor, and non-MAC
commands between them, which set the sweep factor.

% P:mot-idle-beats
\paragraph{Idle beats}
In stock PIM a DRAM column command starts every MAC: HBM-PIM~\cite{hbmpim} executes one instruction per column
command, and AiM~\cite{aimjssc} clocks its PU at its interface's $t_\mathrm{CCD}$. The column commands and their
spacing, \tccds{} and \tccdl, exist to access the DRAM array: each column command drives the bank's column decoder and
I/O sense amplifiers to move one column out of the cell array~\cite{hbmpim}. For a GEMV, the target of these
PUs~\cite{aimjssc,p3llm}, one column meets one input vector, and one beat per column suffices. The slot factor
$\UMAC = A\,b\,\tck / \tslot$ counts the useful beats of one MAC command's slot \tslot: the slot serves $A$ tokens with
$b$ beats each ($b = 1$ on the mobile platform; $b = 2$ on the edge server, where each MAC command feeds a PU from its
two banks). Stock AiM's slot serves one token, so a PU at CK finishes the token's beat in one CK cycle and waits for
the next command (Fig.~\ref{fig:dram_pim}b): three of the slot's $D = 4$ beats idle, and stock AiM's \UMAC is 1/4.
Replacing stock AiM's PU, one beat per column period, with one $D$ times faster, clocked at CK, leaves every stock GEMM
at the same number of cycles: the command spacing, not the PU clock, sets each MAC slot. Idle beats therefore take
three times the time of the useful beats and form the largest segment of every bar in Fig.~\ref{fig:breakdown}:
53.9\,\% of q\_proj's time on the mobile platform and 57.4\,\% on the edge server.

% P:mot-nonmac-commands
\paragraph{Non-MAC commands}
Each row sweep of $c$ MAC slots (Fig.~\ref{fig:dram_pim}c) pays two kinds of non-MAC commands. Its \emph{row-switch
commands}, the two mode switches (TMOD), the row's PREA and ACT16 pair and the WRMAC16, cost the \emph{row-switch
overhead} \trow once per row sweep; its \emph{token load/drain commands}, each token's share of WRGB and its RDMAC16
drain, cost \ttok once per token; refresh adds to both. Stock AiM's row-switch commands outlast the useful MAC work of
their row sweep. Over one decoder layer's weight GEMMs on the mobile platform, where each stock row sweep serves one
token, the simulated row-switch commands take 91.2\,ns per sweep, against 78.0\,ns of useful MAC work, the time
$c\,b\,\tck$ that the reference PU needs for the sweep's beats. The two TMODs take the largest part, 40.5\,ns, and the
ACT16 pair, timed by a simulator preset, takes 37.9\,ns. PREA takes 11.5\,ns and WRMAC16 1.2\,ns. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:breakdown}, the row-switch commands take 19.5\,\% of q\_proj's time on the mobile platform and 13.8\,\%
on the edge server. The token load/drain commands scale with the tokens and the GB fills instead. RDMAC16 drains each
token's sums once per row sweep and takes at most 1.0\,\% of a kernel's time. WRGB fills the GB once per token and $K$
chunk, and one fill serves every row sweep over that chunk: 64 row sweeps in the mobile q\_proj, 16 in the edge-server
q\_proj and one in either one-tile kernel. Token load therefore takes 0.6\,\% and 2.4\,\% of q\_proj's time but
28.3\,\% and 28.5\,\% of the one tile's. Refresh takes 7.1\,\% of q\_proj's time on the mobile platform and 6.2\,\% on
the edge server. On the edge server, stock AiM's non-MAC commands over the layer, 146.2\,ns per row sweep including
41.8\,ns of refresh, outlast its 123.3\,ns of useful MAC work.

% P:mot-sweep-factor
\paragraph{Sweep factor}
The row-switch overhead, the token load/drain and refresh set the sweep factor of a row sweep that serves $A$ tokens:
\begin{equation}
  \fMAC = \frac{(1 - \rho)\, c\, \tslot}{c\, \tslot + \trow + A\, \ttok}, \label{eq:fmac}
\end{equation}

where $\rho$ is the share of time that refresh takes. Fitted on stock AiM at $M = 1$, \trow is 87.73\,ns and \ttok

\subsection{GEMM Support in Prior PIM}
\label{sec:bg:walls}

% P:mot-prior-families
Prior PIM designs fill the idle beats in two families, with more multipliers or with more commands
(Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}). Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline} follows one weight column of one bank through $M = 8$ tokens, in
column periods $T$ of $D = 4$ beats each. Its first three rows, stock AiM and one design of each family, start every
MAC with a command, and stock AiM spends 8 column periods, 8 commands and 8 reads on the 8 tokens.

% P:mot-wider-pu
\paragraph{Wider-PU family}
Six designs widen the MAC step with multipliers. LP-Spec~\cite{lpspec} builds its matrix unit from four 32-wide INT8
ALUs, clocks the ALUs at one MAC step per column period, and shares one unit between two banks; we model one such unit
per bank (Table~\ref{tab:params}). One column command feeds the four ALUs with four tokens, one MAC step each, so the 8
tokens take 2 periods, 2 commands and 2 reads, on four times the stock multipliers. The 4$\times$ ceiling moves the
crossover of this per-bank design with the host to $M = 20$--24 on the mobile platform (Fig.~\ref{fig:roofline}a).
PAPI~\cite{papi} places four processing units in each bank, and StepStone~\cite{stepstone} places SIMD units at the
bank groups or beside the devices of a memory module. Duplex~\cite{duplex} and H$^2$-LLM~\cite{h2llm} build their
multipliers on a logic die under the DRAM, and MoNDE~\cite{monde} builds them into a memory-side controller. We take
LP-Spec~\cite{lpspec} as the representative of the wider-PU family and model it as an \textbf{LP-Spec-style} design,
the rival on the mobile platform (Section~\ref{sec:eval:phone}). LP-Spec-style's slot serves four tokens and fills
every beat, so LP-Spec-style reaches $\UMAC = 1$ (Table~\ref{tab:umac}) only with four times the multipliers: per
multiplier, LP-Spec-style's $U$ is 0.173--0.174 on the mobile platform and 0.185 on the edge server, below stock
AiM's.

% P:mot-logic-cost
\paragraph{Cost of logic in a DRAM die}
Logic in a DRAM die costs area: UPMEM~\cite{devaux} reports that a DRAM process builds logic 10$\times$ less dense
than an ASIC process and routes the logic in three metal layers instead of ten or more. In GDDR6-AiM~\cite{aimjssc} silicon, one PU occupies
0.19\,mm$^2$. Samsung's HBM-PIM~\cite{hbmpim} halves its DRAM sub-arrays to make room for its PUs and so gives up 50\,\% of
the die's memory capacity. By LP-Spec's~\cite{lpspec} own estimate its compute units and buffers take
16.92\,\% of a 16\,Gb LPDDR5 die, against 20--25\,\% of the chip for all of AiM's~\cite{aimjssc} compute circuits.
Four times the multipliers per bank therefore cost capacity, and per unit of logic area the wider-PU family keeps
only part of its 4$\times$ ceiling: LP-Spec-style's ceiling per logic mm$^2$ is 314\,GOPS on the mobile platform and
697 on the edge server, against stock AiM's 216 and 492.

% P:mot-faster-pu
\paragraph{Faster-PU family}
A faster PU paced by per-token commands fills at most two beats per column read. P3-LLM~\cite{p3llm} keeps the weight
of a column read latched in its PU, issues one further command per token and clocks its PU at one beat per HBM2
\tccds{} (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Commands may issue only every \tccds, ``typically a half of
\tccdl{}''~\cite{p3llm}, while the all-bank column runs at \tccdl~\cite{hbmpim}, so at most two tokens share a read:
the 8 tokens take 4 periods, 8 commands and 4 reads. On every memory family whose \tccdl{} is twice \tccds{}, the
depth cap is 2, so the faster-PU family's slot serves at most two tokens and its \UMAC stops at 1/2. The 2$\times$
ceiling moves the crossover of this design with the host to $M = 4$--6 on the edge server (Fig.~\ref{fig:roofline}b).
P3-LLM~\cite{p3llm} is the only design that applies a faster PU to $M > 1$, so we take P3-LLM as the representative of
the faster-PU family and model it as a \textbf{P3-LLM-style} design, the rival on the edge server
(Section~\ref{sec:eval:server}). With the stock multiplier count, P3-LLM-style's ceiling per logic mm$^2$, 390\,GOPS on
the mobile platform and 830 on the edge server, lies above LP-Spec-style's.

% P:mot-rival-row-switch
\paragraph{Row-switch commands in prior designs}
Both families share a row sweep's row-switch commands only among the tokens of one MAC slot, so their non-MAC time
per token falls only as far as the slot widens. LP-Spec-style's row sweep serves the $A = 4$ tokens of its slot and P3-LLM-style's the
$A = 2$ tokens of its slot (Table~\ref{tab:umac}); P3-LLM~\cite{p3llm} reuses each memory access for two tokens to
reduce duplicated row activations. Each design packs its $A$ tokens into one column period, so its row sweep holds
stock AiM's $c$ MAC slots against one set of row-switch commands, and the token load/drain commands of $A$ tokens add
to the sweep. On the mobile platform the non-MAC time per token falls from stock AiM's 123.39\,ns to 63.94\,ns for
P3-LLM-style and 34.41\,ns for LP-Spec-style (Table~\ref{tab:umac}): a row sweep serves more tokens only when the MAC
slot does, with LP-Spec-style's four times the multipliers or P3-LLM-style's one command per token.

% P:mot-other-pim
The remaining LLM PIM designs fall outside the wider-PU and faster-PU families. GEMV-only designs serve one input
vector per weight read~\cite{newton,cent,pimgpt}, lookup-table designs replace multiplications with table
lookups~\cite{localut,pimpal,pimdl}, and scheduling and dataflow designs reorganize the work around the
PU~\cite{pimba,paise,splitsync,dots,pimphony}. Paik et al.~\cite{paik2022} decouple memory access from computation
across banks so that per-bank PIM reaches all-bank throughput on a standard interface. Three designs, the closest prior
art, run several MAC beats per column read, yet each design holds one input vector ($M = 1$): SAL-PIM~\cite{salpim}
clocks its MAC units at twice the column rate, the Precision-Flexible PU~\cite{pfpu} runs four phases per column
interval on packed low-bit operands, and CD-PIM~\cite{cdpim} runs two compute units per bank at twice the internal
clock. CD-PIM~\cite{cdpim} leaves the GEMM to the host processor, and the extra beats of all three designs work on the
same vector. Across the two families and these three designs, a column read feeds several tokens only through added
multipliers or added commands, and a row sweep serves only the tokens of one MAC slot.

\subsection{The Opportunity: DRAM Timing Bounds Access, Not MACs}
\label{sec:bg:depth}

% P:mot-depth
DRAM timing bounds how often a column is read, not how many MACs may consume the column, so one column read can feed
the $D$ beats of Section~\ref{sec:bg:pim}. The PU takes its operands from the I/O sense amplifiers at the bank's I/O
boundary~\cite{hbmpim}, so a column the PU has latched stays usable after its command: P3-LLM~\cite{p3llm} reuses a
latched column for two tokens at unchanged cell-access and column-decoder power, the majority of PIM power. The column
period thus sets how often weights arrive at a PU, and the PU clock sets how often the PU computes on them, so a PU
clocked at CK can spend all $D$ beats of a column period on one latched column.

% P:mot-pu-clock-precedent
\paragraph{PU clock in a DRAM die}
A PU clocked at CK has precedent in DRAM silicon and in a PIM design for HBM2. HBM2's DRAM core runs at a quarter of
the 1.0--1.2\,GHz bus clock, and Samsung's HBM-PIM~\cite{hbmpim} sets its FP16 PU to the core frequency,
250--300\,MHz, in a 20\,nm DRAM process. Logic in later DRAM processes runs faster: UPMEM's processor~\cite{devaux}
is designed to execute 32-bit integer instructions at 500\,MHz in a 2x-nm DRAM process, a deployed UPMEM system
runs the processor at 350\,MHz~\cite{prim}, and GDDR6-AiM~\cite{aimjssc} runs BF16
multipliers and adder trees at 1\,GHz in a 1y-nm DRAM process. The 1y-nm process also builds HBM: Samsung's
HBM2E~\cite{flashbolt} stacks eight 1y-nm DRAM dies. The Precision-Flexible PU~\cite{pfpu} clocks an HBM-PIM-style
PU at CK on HBM2 and splits each 4-\tck column interval into four phases. DRAM periphery has gained logic-process
features: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron build periphery transistors with high-k metal gates~\cite{tiroadmap}, and
1a-nm dies route in five to six metal layers~\cite{tid1a16,tid1a24}.

% P:mot-two-levers
Stock AiM's PU computes one of the $D = 4$ beats that each column read can feed (Fig.~\ref{fig:dram_pim}b), and stock
AiM pays a row sweep's row-switch commands for every token. One lever answers each root cause of
Section~\ref{sec:bg:cmd}. \emph{Beat filling} (Section~\ref{sec:design:beat}) uses all $D$ beats of a column period
without adding multipliers and answers the idle beats, which set the slot factor. \emph{Row reuse}
(Section~\ref{sec:design:reuse}) lets the $V$ tokens of a tile share one row sweep and pay its row-switch commands
once, and answers the non-MAC commands by cutting their time per token.
